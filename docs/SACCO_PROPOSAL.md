> Integrated SACCO Management System

# Introduction

Savings and Credit Cooperative Organizations (SACCOs) occupy a central position in the financial lives of millions of low- and middle-income individuals who lack easy access to formal banking services. A SACCO pools the savings of its members and extends credit back to them at reasonable rates, creating a self-sustaining cycle of financial inclusion that commercial banks often overlook. Because SACCOs operate on member trust and mutual benefit rather than pure profit, they carry significant social and economic weight in the communities they serve: they finance small businesses, fund education and medical expenses, and provide a disciplined savings culture that formal banks rarely cultivate at the grassroots level.

Despite this importance, many SACCOs still run their day-to-day operations through manual, paper-based processes or, at best, fragmented spreadsheet systems. Member registration forms sit in physical filing cabinets, loan applications pass through several desks before approval, and financial statements are compiled by hand at the end of each reporting period. This exposes SACCOs to considerable risk: misplaced records, calculation errors, delayed services, and an inability to produce timely, accurate reports for members, management, or regulators.

This project proposes the design and development of an Integrated SACCO Management System (ISMS): a multi-tenant Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platform that onboards multiple independent SACCO organizations onto a single, shared instance of the software rather than a one-off, single-institution deployment. The platform is built on a NestJS (Node.js/TypeScript) REST API backend following a Clean/Multi-Layered Architecture, over a shared PostgreSQL relational database in which every tenant SACCO\'s data lives in the same set of tables, isolated from every other tenant by a tenant_id column enforced through PostgreSQL Row-Level Security (RLS) policies rather than by provisioning a separate database per SACCO. The REST API is consumed by two client channels --- a Next.js/React frontend (styled with Tailwind CSS and backed by React Query for server-state management), serving distinct Admin, Staff, and Member web portals, and a USSD short-code menu (dialed as \*812#) that extends basic account inquiry and transaction-notification access to members on ordinary GSM feature phones. The system consolidates member management, savings and share administration on a closed-loop double-entry accounting ledger, loan processing governed by a defined savings-to-loan collateral multiplier, mobile money and SMS/WhatsApp gateway integration, document generation, and reporting into a single, coherent platform that scales economically across many tenant SACCOs on shared infrastructure. Member identity verification during registration is designed to integrate with Ethiopia\'s Fayda National ID system, so that a member\'s identity is authenticated against a national digital ID rather than a locally issued, unverifiable paper document.

# Organizational Background

SACCOs are member-owned financial cooperatives registered and regulated under cooperative societies\' legislation. Each SACCO is governed by an elected board of directors and managed by an administrative staff that typically includes a manager, loan officers, tellers or cashiers, and a records/accounts clerk. Members join by purchasing shares and subsequently build savings accounts, from which they may later borrow at agreed interest rates and repayment schedules, generally bounded by a savings-based collateral multiplier that determines how large a loan a member\'s existing savings balance can support.

This proposal targets the general operational model shared by most small to medium-sized SACCOs rather than a single named institution, so that the resulting system remains adaptable across different cooperative societies with similar structures. A typical target SACCO manages between several hundred and a few thousand active members, offers standard savings and share accounts alongside short- and medium-term loan products, and currently relies on manual registers, spreadsheets, or disconnected desktop tools to track these operations. A meaningful share of that membership is rural or otherwise without reliable smartphone or broadband internet access, which is the specific constraint that motivates the USSD channel described throughout this proposal: a member does not need a smartphone, a data plan, or even literacy in a particular script to check a balance or receive a transaction confirmation over \*812#.

## Statement of the Problem

The current manual and semi-manual approach to SACCO management produces a range of operational problems that directly affect service quality and organizational integrity. The development team identifies the following core issues:

**Inefficient member registration and record-keeping.** Staff record member details on paper forms or in disconnected spreadsheets, which makes it difficult to search, update, or verify member information quickly. Because there is no authoritative external identity check at registration, duplicate records and identity fraud (a single individual registering under slightly different name spellings to obtain multiple loans) are both possible and, in practice, difficult to detect after the fact.

**Slow and error-prone loan processing.** Loan officers manually calculate interest, manually check a member\'s savings balance against the SACCO\'s lending policy (commonly a fixed multiple of savings, such as a 3x savings-to-loan ceiling), and manually cross-check repayment history before approving new credit. This manual arithmetic introduces calculation errors and delays that frustrate members and increase the SACCO\'s exposure to over-lending against insufficient collateral.

**Poor document preparation and management.** SACCOs must regularly generate member statements, loan agreements, receipts, and share certificates. Producing these documents by hand consumes significant staff time and produces inconsistent formatting, which undermines the SACCO\'s professional standing with members and auditors alike.

**Limited reporting capability.** Management and the board require accurate, up-to-date financial reports to guide decision-making and satisfy regulatory obligations. Manual compilation of these reports delays strategic decisions and increases the likelihood of reporting inaccuracies, since figures are re-keyed by hand from ledger books rather than aggregated directly from a single transactional source.

**Weak data security, access control, and continuity.** Paper records and unsecured spreadsheets are vulnerable to loss through fire, water damage, misplacement, or unauthorized access, and there is no role-based restriction on who may view or alter a given record. The absence of a structured access-control matrix and an immutable audit trail creates opportunities for fraudulent alteration of financial records that would otherwise be immediately detectable.

**Absence of a single source of truth and of an accessible channel for members without smartphones.** Because different departments (registration, savings, loans, accounts) often maintain separate records, the SACCO cannot easily reconcile a member\'s complete financial position, which slows customer service and internal auditing. Members without a smartphone or reliable data connectivity, meanwhile, have no self-service channel at all and must travel to the branch even for a simple balance inquiry.

## Project General Objective

The general objective of this project is to design and develop an Integrated SACCO Management System

--- a multi-tenant SaaS platform serving multiple SACCO organizations on shared infrastructure, built on a NestJS (Node.js/TypeScript) REST API backend (Clean/Multi-Layered Architecture) over a shared PostgreSQL database secured with tenant_id-scoped Row-Level Security, exposed through REST endpoints to a Next.js/React web application (Admin, Staff, and Member portals) and a \*812# USSD menu --- that automates member registration (with Fayda National ID verification), savings and share management on a double-entry ledger, loan processing governed by an automated savings-multiplier eligibility check, mobile money and SMS/WhatsApp gateway integration, document generation, and financial reporting for Savings and Credit Cooperative Organizations.

## Specific Objectives

To achieve the general objective, the project pursues the following specific objectives, each tied to a concrete technical artifact rather than a general intention:

-   To analyze the existing manual SACCO operations and document the functional and non-functional requirements of the proposed system, producing a Software Requirement Specification (SRS), a User Story Map covering each actor\'s primary journeys, and interview and observation records.

-   To design a normalized relational database, expressed as an Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD) and an accompanying PostgreSQL Data Definition Language (DDL) script, capable of storing member profiles, account records, loan records, double-entry ledger transactions, and generated documents.

-   To develop a member registration and profile management module, exposed through REST endpoints, that allows staff to create, update, search, and retrieve member records efficiently, including a Fayda National ID verification call at the point of registration.

-   To develop a savings and share management module that records deposits, withdrawals, and share purchases as balanced double-entry ledger postings, and that calculates account balances and the savings-multiplier loan ceiling automatically.

-   To develop a loan management module that handles loan application, automated eligibility assessment against the savings multiplier and guarantor pledge rules, approval workflow, disbursement, interest calculation, and repayment tracking, all posted as atomic, balanced ledger transactions.

-   To develop a document generation module that automatically produces member statements, loan agreements, receipts, and share certificates in a standardized format from the underlying ledger data.

-   To develop a reporting module, accessible from the Next.js Web Application, that generates real-time financial and operational reports for management and regulatory review directly from the ledger rather than from a manually reconciled figure.

-   To implement a Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) security matrix that restricts system functions and REST endpoints according to staff responsibilities, and to log every state-changing action to an immutable audit trail.

-   To implement a USSD channel (short code \*812#) offering balance inquiry, mini-statement, and transaction-notification access for members on GSM feature phones, built against the same REST API and ledger data as the Next.js web application.

-   To design and implement a multi-tenant SaaS architecture in which multiple SACCO organizations share a single PostgreSQL database and application deployment, with tenant_id-scoped Row-Level Security policies enforcing strict data isolation between tenants, and a Super Admin console for platform-level tenant provisioning and oversight.

-   To integrate mobile money gateways (Customer-to-Business and Business-to-Customer) for savings deposits, loan repayments, and loan disbursements, and SMS/WhatsApp gateways for transactional notifications and one-time passwords, each exposed behind a provider-agnostic wrapper service.

-   To test the system thoroughly against the documented requirements, producing automated unit test scripts, an integration test suite, a test case matrix, and signed User Acceptance Testing (UAT) sign-off sheets.

-   To produce complete system documentation, including a deployment runbook and user manuals, to support long-term maintenance and staff training.

## Feasibility of the Project

### Technical Feasibility

The project\'s technology stack was selected specifically for reliability and for the availability of skilled support in the target deployment environment, not merely for developer familiarity. On the backend, the system is built as a NestJS (Node.js/TypeScript) REST API, a mature, modular backend framework that exposes a versioned REST API over HTTPS to every client channel; this keeps the Application Logic Layer\'s business rules (savings-multiplier eligibility, double-entry posting, RBAC enforcement) in one place regardless of which channel initiated the request. The persistent store is PostgreSQL, a fully ACID-compliant open-source relational database management system capable of enforcing foreign-key referential integrity, check constraints (such as a non-negative balance constraint), and row-level locking for atomic transactions --- properties that a spreadsheet or a NoSQL document store cannot guarantee to the same degree, and that a closed-loop financial ledger requires.

On the client side, the Next.js Web Application is a React front end built with Next.js, a browser-based single-page application that consumes the REST API and requires only a standard desktop computer with a modern browser, already present in the target SACCO\'s office per Section 1.1.1. The USSD channel, accessed by dialing \*812#, requires no smartphone, data plan, or app installation at all: it operates over

the existing GSM signalling channel through a licensed USSD aggregator or gateway, which relays each menu selection to the same REST API as a short synchronous HTTP request. No specialized hardware is required beyond a local or cloud-hosted application server and a fixed-line or mobile internet connection sufficient for REST traffic, which keeps the technical risk low.

### Economic Feasibility

The cost of developing and deploying the system --- an open-source database engine, a free-to-license backend framework, and a shared cloud hosting footprint sized for the platform operator\'s aggregate tenant base rather than a server per SACCO --- is modest relative to the recurring losses SACCOs currently sustain from manual errors, staff overtime, and delayed loan processing, and it is further reduced for each individual SACCO tenant because that hosting cost is amortized across every tenant on the shared platform rather than borne by one institution alone. USSD aggregator fees are typically charged per session or per SMS-equivalent unit rather than requiring capital investment, so the member-facing channel scales with usage rather than with up-front cost. Once deployed, the system

reduces the labor hours required for document preparation and reporting, which yields a favorable long-term cost-benefit position for each onboarded SACCO.

### Operational Feasibility

SACCO staff already use basic computer applications such as spreadsheets and word processors, which indicates that they can adapt to a structured Next.js Web Application with reasonable training; its form-based interaction patterns mirror the paper forms staff already use. On the member side, dialing a short USSD code and navigating a numbered menu is a pattern already familiar to GSM subscribers from mobile-money and airtime-recharge services, which minimizes the learning curve for the member-facing channel without requiring any new literacy beyond what members already exercise for those existing services.

### Schedule Feasibility

The project follows a phased development plan (requirement analysis, design, implementation, testing, and deployment), detailed fully in Section 1.1.10, that fits within the internship timeframe allotted for this task. Each phase produces a concrete, verifiable deliverable --- a signed SRS, an ERD and DDL script, a working module, a signed UAT sheet, a deployment runbook --- which allows progress to be tracked and adjusted if delays occur rather than discovered only at the final delivery date.

## Significance of the Project

The proposed system delivers value to multiple stakeholders. It reduces the administrative burden on SACCO staff by automating repetitive calculations and document preparation, which frees staff time for member service. It improves the accuracy and timeliness of financial records by posting every movement as a balanced, atomic double-entry transaction, which strengthens the SACCO\'s credibility with members, auditors, and regulatory bodies. It provides management with real-time reporting that supports faster, better-informed decisions regarding loan policy, liquidity, and growth planning. It establishes a secure, centralized data store, protected by an RBAC security matrix and an immutable audit log, against loss, duplication, and unauthorized alteration. Finally, a USSD channel is designed into the architecture to extend service to members without smartphones, closing a financial-inclusion gap that a web-only system would leave open; delivering this channel in a live pilot depends on securing short-code access through a licensed telecom aggregator, so it is documented in this proposal as a future-work item rather than a current-phase deliverable (see Out-of-Scope Elements, Section 1.1.7).

## Beneficiaries of the Project

-   SACCO Management and the Board of Directors, who gain accurate, timely reports for strategic and regulatory decision-making.

-   SACCO Staff (tellers, loan officers, records clerks), who perform their daily tasks faster and with fewer errors through the Next.js Web Application.

## Methodology

The project follows an Object-Oriented Analysis and Design (OOAD) methodology, which aligns naturally with the SACCO domain\'s real-world entities (members, accounts, loans, transactions) and supports the subsequent development of use case diagrams, sequence diagrams, and class diagrams, all captured as concrete artifacts rather than left as narrative description.

The development team gathers requirements through the following data collection methods, each producing its own documented artifact:

**Interviews** with SACCO managers, loan officers, and tellers to understand current workflows and pain points, recorded as structured interview transcripts that feed directly into the User Story Map.

**Document analysis** of existing registration forms, loan application forms, and financial statement templates to capture the data fields and business rules (including the SACCO\'s specific savings-multiplier ceiling) the system must support.

**Direct observation** of SACCO front-office and back-office operations to identify inefficiencies that interview responses alone may not reveal, recorded as observation notes referenced during use case validation.

**Questionnaires,** where appropriate, to gather structured feedback from a broader sample of staff and members regarding service quality, desired improvements, and comfort with a USSD self-service channel.

For design and modeling, the team produces Unified Modeling Language (UML) artifacts: use case diagrams, sequence diagrams, class diagrams, and Entity-Relationship Diagrams (ERDs). For implementation, the team uses a NestJS (Node.js/TypeScript) REST API backend, a PostgreSQL relational database, a REST API contract documented in an OpenAPI specification, a browser-based Next.js Web Application, and a USSD menu script integrated through a licensed USSD gateway.

## Scope of the Project

### In-Scope Functional Modules

The in-scope functionality is organized into six groups: platform and multi-tenant administration, core business logic, security and access control, interface/channel modules, external payment and messaging integrations, and reporting and compliance. Each group is implemented as a distinct component behind

the REST API, so that a change to one (for example, adding a new report) does not require touching the others.

**Platform and multi-tenant administration modules** cover onboarding and provisioning of new tenant SACCO organizations onto the shared platform, a Super Admin console for cross-tenant oversight, and enforcement of tenant_id-scoped PostgreSQL Row-Level Security so that one tenant\'s data is never visible to another, on a single shared database rather than a database-per-tenant deployment.

**Core business logic modules** cover member registration and profile management (including Fayda National ID verification at intake); savings and share account operations, including automated computation of the savings-based loan-eligibility ceiling under the SACCO\'s savings multiplier policy (for example, a 3x multiplier, meaning a member may qualify for a loan of up to three times their qualifying savings balance, subject to guarantor pledge rules); and loan processing from application through disbursement and repayment, with every monetary movement posted as a balanced double-entry ledger transaction rather than a single balance update.

**Security and access modules** cover staff authentication, a Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) security matrix that restricts each REST endpoint to the staff roles authorized to call it, and an immutable audit log that records every create, update, approval, and disbursement action with a timestamp and the acting staff member\'s identity.

**Interface / channel modules** cover the Next.js web application --- presenting separate Admin, Staff (Teller Desk), and Member portal experiences --- used by tellers, loan officers, managers, and auditors, and the USSD self-service menu dialed as \*812#, offering balance inquiry, mini-statement retrieval, and loan-status inquiry for members over ordinary GSM handsets, each channel calling the same underlying REST API so that business rules are enforced identically regardless of entry point.

**External payment and messaging integration modules** cover mobile money gateway integration for Customer-to-Business (member-initiated deposits and loan repayments) and Business-to-Customer (loan disbursements and withdrawals to mobile wallet) transaction flows, and SMS/WhatsApp gateway integration for transactional notifications and one-time passwords, each mediated through a provider-agnostic wrapper service.

**Reporting and compliance modules** cover on-demand generation of member statements, loan agreements, receipts, and share certificates in a standardized format, together with financial and operational reports (loan portfolio summary, savings summary, trial balance derived directly from the ledger) for management and regulatory review.

### Out-of-Scope Elements

To keep the project achievable within the internship timeframe, three categories of functionality are explicitly excluded from the current phase, each for a specific, stated reason rather than by omission.

**Hardware and physical infrastructure** such as procurement of point-of-sale terminals, receipt printers, card-issuance equipment, or dedicated server hardware are out of scope; the project assumes the SACCO already possesses, or will separately procure, standard office computers and either a local server or a cloud hosting account.

**Biometric authentication** such as fingerprint enrollment or facial-recognition verification of members at the branch counter is out of scope for this phase. Identity verification instead relies on the Fayda

National ID system\'s verification service at the point of registration, which is sufficient to eliminate duplicate and fraudulent registrations without requiring the SACCO to procure and maintain biometric capture hardware.

**Interbank clearing and settlement limits** mean the system integrates with mobile money gateways (M-Pesa, Telebirr, and partner bank channels) for member-facing Customer-to-Business deposits/repayments and Business-to-Customer disbursements/withdrawals, as detailed in Chapter Five, but it does not implement direct interbank settlement rails itself: it does not participate in interbank Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) or automated clearing house processes, and it does not act as a settlement bank for other financial institutions. The mobile money integration is limited to initiating and receiving webhook-confirmed gateway transactions against the SACCO\'s own ledger; reconciliation of the gateway provider\'s own settlement with its underlying bank remains the provider\'s responsibility, not the platform\'s.

**Live third-party channel integrations for USSD and mobile money are designed at the architecture and API-contract level (Chapter Five) but are not implemented against live provider endpoints in the current internship phase, since USSD short-code allocation and mobile money merchant onboarding both depend on approval timelines held by external telecom operators and regulators, not on development effort. Similarly, transactional notifications in the current phase are delivered by email (via Nodemailer over SMTP) rather than through the SMS/WhatsApp gateway wrapper described in Section 5.1.3; that section documents the target production design, which the current phase substitutes with a lower-friction channel appropriate to pilot scale. Fayda National ID verification (Section 1.1.8, FR-1.1/FR-1.2) is the one external integration implemented against a live endpoint in this phase, since it is accessible through a public developer portal without a commercial gatekeeper.**

## Limitations of the Project

Beyond the deliberately out-of-scope elements above, the system operates under four operational constraints inherent to its architecture and its deployment environment, which the development team documents explicitly so that stakeholders set expectations accordingly.

**Network infrastructure dependency.** Both client channels depend on network availability: the Next.js web application requires a working internet connection to the shared, multi-tenant application server for every function outside the Teller Desk\'s offline-first outbox queue (Section 1.1.9\'s Offline synchronization note below), and the USSD channel depends entirely on the mobile network operator\'s GSM signalling network and the availability of the USSD aggregator\'s gateway. Where GSM coverage is weak or the aggregator\'s platform is degraded, \*812# sessions will fail to establish or will time out, and the system has no fallback channel for that member during the outage.

**USSD device and display constraints.** USSD is a session-based, text-only protocol with a small per-screen character budget (commonly around 182 characters per menu page across most GSM networks and handsets) and a short session timeout (typically on the order of 120 to 180 seconds of inactivity before the session is dropped by the network). This constrains the USSD menu to short, numbered options and terse balance or mini-statement text rather than the richer tables and documents the Next.js web application can render, and it means a member who pauses too long mid-session must redial and navigate the menu again from the start.

**Interbank clearing boundaries.** While mobile money Customer-to-Business and Business-to-Customer flows are in scope (Section 1.1.8.1) and integrated behind webhook-confirmed gateway callbacks (Chapter Five), direct interbank Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) and automated clearing house participation remain explicitly out of scope (Section 1.1.8.2). Any inflow or outflow that involves a bank or another financial institution outside the SACCO\'s mobile money gateway relationships must still be reconciled manually against a bank statement; the system can record that such a transfer occurred once confirmed, but it cannot itself initiate, confirm, or clear an interbank settlement leg.

**Offline synchronization limits.** The two client channels differ in their tolerance for connectivity loss. USSD has no offline mode at all, and none is possible: it is inherently a live, session-based GSM protocol with no local caching, so a member\'s \*812# session simply fails to establish or drops when connectivity is unavailable. The web application\'s Teller Desk, by contrast, is built offline-first (detailed in Chapter Four): it uses an IndexedDB-backed outbox to queue transactions locally, with idempotency keys and a background synchronization worker that drains the queue once connectivity returns, so a remote-

branch teller can continue recording deposits and withdrawals through a network outage rather than having all transaction posting stop. This offline-first behavior is scoped specifically to the Teller Desk; the Admin, Manager, and Member-facing portions of the web application, and the USSD channel, still require a live connection for every operation, and any conflict that can only be resolved server-side (for example, two offline tellers both drawing down the same account) is rejected individually on sync and routed back to the originating device as a reviewable exception.

## Project Deliverables and Phases

The project is organized into five sequential phases. Each phase below is described individually, together with the exact technical artifacts it produces, rather than summarized only in a table.

### Phase 1: Requirement Analysis

This phase gathers and documents what the system must do before any design or code is produced. The development team conducts structured interviews with tellers, loan officers, and the manager; performs document analysis of existing registration forms, loan application forms, and financial statement templates; and directly observes front-office and back-office operations. The phase concludes with three concrete deliverables: a complete Software Requirement Specification (SRS) document (Chapter Two of this proposal), a User Story Map that traces each actor\'s end-to-end journeys (for example, a member\'s journey from applying for a loan through receiving disbursement notification over USSD), and a signed record of interview and observation sessions that supports later traceability between a requirement and the stakeholder who raised it.

### Phase 2: System Design

This phase translates the SRS into a concrete architecture and data model (Chapter Three of this proposal). It produces a 3-tier architecture diagram distinguishing the Presentation Layer (Next.js Web Application and USSD menu), the Application Logic Layer (NestJS REST API implementing the RBAC matrix, the savings-multiplier eligibility check, and double-entry ledger posting), and the Data Access Layer (PostgreSQL). It also produces UML use case diagrams, sequence diagrams for the highest-complexity flows (loan application and disbursement), a refined business class diagram, a full Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD) with an accompanying PostgreSQL DDL script defining every table, primary key, foreign key, and check constraint, an OpenAPI specification documenting every REST endpoint and its request/response schema, and the RBAC security matrix mapping each staff role to the specific endpoints it may call.

### Phase 3: Implementation

This phase builds the system across three parallel workstreams rather than one module at a time, since the team of six splits into Backend, Frontend/Gateways, and QA/Offline Sync & UAT tracks working the same weeks (Section 5.3). The Backend workstream implements the Member Management module (including the Fayda National ID verification call), the Savings and Shares module (including automated savings-multiplier calculation), the Loan and Credit Management module (including automated eligibility assessment, approval workflow, and double-entry ledger posting on disbursement and repayment), the Document and Reporting Engine, and the Security and Audit module (RBAC enforcement and audit logging) as REST endpoints on the NestJS backend and PostgreSQL database. Concurrently, the Frontend workstream implements the Next.js Web Application as a REST API consumer, and the USSD menu scripts for the \*812# short code (designed against the USSD gateway/aggregator contract; see Out-of-Scope Elements, Section 1.1.7, for the current phase\'s implementation status). Each module is committed to source control individually and accompanied by its own set of automated unit test scripts, with the QA workstream validating modules as they land rather than waiting for the full build to complete.

### Phase 4: Testing

This phase verifies the implemented system against the requirements documented in Phase 1. It produces a test case matrix tracing each functional requirement to at least one test case; an automated unit test suite covering the business-logic layer, in particular the double-entry balancing rule (a transaction is rejected if its debit and credit legs do not sum to zero) and the savings-multiplier eligibility calculation; an integration test suite exercising the full REST API against a test PostgreSQL instance, including the USSD gateway\'s request/response contract; and a User Acceptance Testing (UAT) round conducted with representative SACCO staff, concluding in signed UAT sign-off sheets that record which use cases were accepted, which produced defects, and how each defect was resolved before deployment.

### Phase 5: Deployment and Documentation

This final phase moves the tested system into its production environment and hands it over for long-term operation. It produces a deployment runbook documenting the exact steps to provision the PostgreSQL database, deploy the NestJS REST API, configure the React (Next.js) Next.js Web Application, and register the \*812# short code with the USSD aggregator; a system administrator manual covering RBAC role configuration, backup scheduling, and audit log review; an end-user manual for tellers, loan officers, and managers covering the Next.js Web Application\'s screens; and a final project report consolidating the outcomes of all preceding phases.

## Work Breakdown and Coordination

The project divides into overlapping tasks running concurrently across the team of six rather than in a single sequential pass: requirement gathering and analysis, database and API design, module-by-module implementation (member management, savings management, loan management, the double-entry ledger, document generation, reporting, the USSD channel), integration testing, and final documentation. Each pair of team members owns one workstream (Backend, Frontend/Gateways, or QA/Offline Sync & UAT, per Section 1.1.13) and takes responsibility for the modules within it, coordinating through regular progress meetings, tracked against the phase deliverables in Section 1.1.10 and the weekly breakdown in Section 5.3, to ensure the three workstreams integrate cleanly into a single system behind the shared REST API contract.

## Motivation

Financial inclusion remains an area where software can deliver a disproportionately large social benefit relative to its development cost. SACCOs serve populations that formal banks frequently underserve, yet these cooperatives often operate with the least technological support, and a meaningful share of their members have no channel at all beyond a physical branch visit. Extending a self-service USSD channel alongside a properly engineered, ledger-backed core system offers the opportunity to apply software engineering skills to a domain with tangible community impact, while providing hands-on experience with the complete software development lifecycle, from requirement analysis through deployment.

# Business Area Analysis

## Detailed Analysis

### Purpose of the System

The Integrated SACCO Management System (ISMS) automates the core financial and administrative operations of a Savings and Credit Cooperative Organization. It replaces manual registers and disconnected spreadsheets with a single, centralized NestJS (Node.js/TypeScript) REST API backend over a PostgreSQL database, exposed through REST endpoints to a Next.js Web Application for staff and a

\*812# USSD menu for members. It manages member registration (with Fayda National ID verification), savings and share accounts governed by a savings-multiplier eligibility rule, loan processing posted as balanced double-entry ledger transactions, document generation, and financial reporting. This document specifies the functional and non-functional requirements that the system must satisfy, and it provides the foundation for the design in Chapter Three.

### Objectives and Success Criteria of the Project

The system\'s success is measured against the specific objectives already stated in Section 1.1.3 of the Project Proposal. Concretely, the project succeeds if: member registration time drops from the current manual baseline to a few minutes per member, including Fayda National ID verification; loan processing time from application to decision is reduced, automatically checked against the savings multiplier, and fully auditable; documents (statements, agreements, receipts) generate automatically and consistently; management can produce an accurate, ledger-derived financial report on demand rather than after days of manual compilation; and a member without a smartphone can retrieve a balance or mini-statement over \*812# without visiting the branch.

### General Description

The ISMS is a role-based, database-backed, multi-tenant SaaS application accessed over the internet through a Next.js Web Application by staff and members of any onboarded SACCO tenant, and through a USSD short code for members on feature phones. Each user class listed in Section 2.1.1.5 interacts with a subset of the system\'s functions according to their role, enforced through the RBAC security matrix detailed in Section 2.6.2, and every interaction is additionally scoped to that user\'s tenant via the tenant_id and Row-Level Security model described in Section 2.5.7. At a functional level, the system organizes around five cooperating modules --- Member Management, Savings and Share Management, Loan Management, Document Generation, and Reporting --- plus a Security and Audit module and a Channel Integration module (Next.js Web Application and USSD).

## Current (Existing) System Description

As established in the Project Proposal (Section 1.1.1, Statement of the Problem), the SACCO\'s existing system consists of manual paper registers supplemented in places by disconnected spreadsheets maintained independently by different staff members. Member registration forms are filed physically with no independent identity check; savings transactions are recorded in a passbook and a ledger book simultaneously, requiring manual double entry; loan applications circulate as paper forms between the loan officer and manager for approval, with eligibility against the savings multiplier checked by hand using a calculator; and financial reports are compiled manually at month-end by aggregating figures from the ledger books. There is no automated interest calculation, no centralized member record, no member self-service channel, and no audit trail beyond the physical paper documents themselves.

## Proposed System

The proposed ISMS replaces each manual step identified above with an automated equivalent while preserving the underlying business process the SACCO already follows, so that staff can transition to the new system without relearning their core responsibilities. Member records move from paper files to a searchable PostgreSQL-backed database, with identity confirmed against the Fayda National ID system at registration rather than trusted on the face of a paper form. Savings transactions post directly as balanced double-entry ledger entries against a single account record, eliminating manual double-entry bookkeeping. Loan applications route electronically from submission through an automated savings-multiplier eligibility check, approval, and disbursement, with every step timestamped, attributable to a specific staff member, and posted atomically. Documents and reports generate on demand from the same underlying ledger data, removing the month-end manual compilation bottleneck entirely, and members without smartphones gain a \*812# self-service channel where none existed before.

# Sequence Diagramming

The sequence diagrams model the internal object interactions behind the two representative use cases with the most cross-object coordination. The Apply for Loan sequence traces the call from the Loan Officer through the LoanController REST endpoint, to the LoanService (which queries AccountService for the savings balance, applies the savings-multiplier rule, and checks GuarantorService for any pledged collateral), and finally to the repository layer that persists the resulting Loan record in PostgreSQL. The Deposit Savings sequence traces the simpler pattern shared by most account operations: Teller through the AccountController, to the AccountService, which constructs a balanced pair of LedgerEntry objects and persists both the Transaction and its two LedgerEntry rows atomically within a single database transaction, so that a failure partway through leaves no unbalanced entry behind.

# Conceptual Modeling

## Refined Business Class Diagram

The conceptual class diagram identifies the core business classes the system must persist and the relationships between them. Member owns one or more Account records (both SavingsAccount and ShareAccount specialize the general Account class in the full design). Staff process Loan applications, which reference LoanGuarantor pledges against a guarantor\'s Account, and record Transactions that each resolve into two balanced LedgerEntry rows. Each Loan carries a schedule of LoanRepayment records. Transactions and Accounts produce Document and Report outputs on demand.

## Activity Diagramming

The activity diagram traces the control flow of the loan approval process end to end: application submission, the automated savings-multiplier and guarantor-pledge eligibility check as the process\'s key decision point, routing to the loan officer or manager depending on amount, and either disbursement (approved path, posted as a balanced ledger transaction) or a rejection notification delivered to the member, which may itself be surfaced over the \*812# loan-status menu.

## User Interface Prototyping --- Navigational Paths and Screen Mock-ups

The Next.js Web Application follows a consistent navigational pattern: a persistent side navigation panel on the left for switching between modules (Dashboard, Members, Savings, Loans, Reports, Administration), and a main content panel on the right showing the active screen --- for example, the loan officer\'s view after entering applicant details, with the system\'s automated savings-multiplier eligibility check displayed inline before submission. # Functional Requirements Specification

## Member Management Requirements

-   FR-1.1: The system shall verify a new member\'s identity against the Fayda National ID service before creating a member profile.

-   FR-1.2: The system shall reject registration if the presented Fayda National ID number is already linked to an existing active member profile.

-   FR-1.3: The system shall allow authorized staff to search, update, and retrieve a member profile by member number, Fayda National ID number, or name.

## Savings and Shares Requirements

-   FR-2.1: The system shall record every deposit and withdrawal as a balanced double-entry ledger transaction against the member\'s account.

-   FR-2.2: The system shall compute a member\'s loan-eligibility ceiling as the member\'s qualifying savings balance multiplied by the SACCO\'s configured savings multiplier (for example, 3x).

-   FR-2.3: The system shall prevent a withdrawal that would reduce an account\'s available balance below zero or below any amount currently held as pledged loan collateral.

## Loan Management Requirements

-   FR-3.1: The system shall calculate a loan application\'s preliminary eligibility automatically by comparing the requested principal against the applicant\'s savings-multiplier ceiling plus any pledged guarantor collateral.

-   FR-3.2: The system shall route loan applications above a configured principal threshold to the Manager role for approval, and applications at or below that threshold to the Loan Officer role.

-   FR-3.3: The system shall post every loan disbursement and repayment as an atomic, balanced double-entry ledger transaction; a transaction whose debit and credit legs do not sum to zero shall be rejected in full rather than partially committed.

## Document and Reporting Requirements

-   FR-4.1: The system shall generate a member statement, loan agreement, receipt, or share certificate in a standardized template on demand from the current ledger data.

-   FR-4.2: The system shall generate a financial report (loan portfolio summary, savings summary, trial balance) for a specified reporting period by aggregating ledger entries directly, without manual re-entry of figures.

## Security and Audit Requirements

-   FR-5.1: The system shall authenticate every staff user and authorize each REST endpoint call against the RBAC security matrix defined in Section 2.6.2.

-   FR-5.2: The system shall record every create, update, approval, and disbursement action to an immutable audit log, including the acting staff member\'s identity and a timestamp.

## Availability and Reliability Requirements

The shared application-server cluster and PostgreSQL database shall target availability suited to serving many tenant SACCOs across whatever office hours and time zones they individually keep, rather than a single tenant\'s office hours, with a documented backup schedule (Section 3.5.2) rather than a formal high-availability service level agreement, since a full active-active cluster is out of the technical and economic feasibility bounds established in Section 1.1.4.

## Usability Requirements

The Next.js Web Application shall follow form-based interaction patterns familiar to staff already using spreadsheets and word processors. The USSD menu shall keep every screen within the network\'s per-page character budget, use plain numbered options with no reliance on special characters unsupported by common feature-phone handsets, and re-confirm any monetary action (for example, a loan-status inquiry showing an outstanding balance) in unambiguous, non-abbreviated currency figures.

## Compliance and Regulatory Requirements

The system shall retain ledger entries and the audit log indefinitely, or for the minimum period required by the applicable cooperative societies\' legislation, whichever is longer, and shall never allow a posted ledger entry to be edited or deleted; a correction is recorded as a new, reversing entry rather than a modification of the original, preserving a complete and tamper-evident financial history for regulatory review.

## Multi-Tenancy and Data Isolation Requirements

Every table in the schema (Section 3.4.3) carries a tenant_id column, and PostgreSQL Row-Level Security policies scope every query, insert, and update to the session\'s current tenant context, so that cross-tenant data isolation is enforced at the database engine itself rather than relying solely on discipline in the NestJS Application Logic Layer\'s query-building code. A staff user\'s session is bound to exactly one tenant_id for the duration of that session; only the Super Admin actor operates outside this per-tenant scoping, and then only through the separate, step-up-authenticated platform administration surface described in Section 2.1.1.5, never through the tenant-scoped staff RBAC roles listed in Section 2.6.2.

# Introduction and Architectural Overview

## Purpose of This Chapter

This chapter translates the requirements and conceptual class model established in Chapter Two into a concrete system design. It defines the NestJS REST API architecture the Integrated SACCO Management System (ISMS) follows, decomposes the system into cooperating subsystems, specifies how the conceptual business classes map onto a shared, tenant_id-scoped PostgreSQL relational schema, documents the REST API contract shared by both client channels, and describes the shared multi-tenant deployment and data-protection arrangements appropriate for a SaaS platform serving many SACCO organizations.

## Architectural Style Selection

The ISMS adopts a Clean/Multi-Layered Architecture, implemented as a single NestJS REST API solution rather than a distributed microservice architecture, since the system serves many SACCO organizations as tenants on a single shared multi-tenant SaaS deployment rather than a large, geographically distributed fleet of independently operated services, and the development team is small. A layered architecture keeps the presentation, business rules, and data storage concerns separated and independently maintainable, without introducing the operational overhead of network calls between separately deployed services, while the shared-database, tenant_id-scoped multi-tenancy model (Section 2.6.6) lets every tenant SACCO run on the same application and database instances rather than a dedicated deployment per SACCO. The REST API published by the Application Logic Layer is the single integration point for both client channels: the Next.js Web Application and the \*812# USSD gateway call the same versioned, tenant-scoped endpoints, so the savings-multiplier rule, the RBAC matrix, the Row-Level Security tenant isolation, and the double-entry ledger logic are implemented and enforced exactly once.

# Subsystem / Component Decomposition

## Decomposition Overview

Within the Application Logic Layer, the system decomposes into six cooperating subsystems, each corresponding to a functional module introduced in the Project Proposal and elaborated into use cases and requirements in Chapter Two. Each subsystem owns a specific set of business classes and exposes a narrow set of REST endpoints, while the Security and Audit subsystem cuts across all of them rather than standing apart as an isolated module.

## Member Management Subsystem

This subsystem owns the Member class and its associated profile data. It implements member registration, the Fayda National ID verification call (an outbound HTTPS request to the national identity service\'s REST endpoint, validated before the member profile is persisted), profile search and update, and the creation of the initial share account described in the Register Member use case (Section 2.2.6.1). It exposes endpoints such as POST /api/members, GET /api/members/{id}, and GET

/api/members?search=.

## Savings and Shares Subsystem

This subsystem owns the Account class and its specializations, SavingsAccount and ShareAccount. It implements deposits, withdrawals, share purchases, automatic balance calculation, and the computation of the savings-multiplier loan-eligibility ceiling (qualifying savings balance × configured multiplier, for example 3x) exposed to the Loan and Credit Management subsystem. It also maintains

the held-balance status on a savings account when that balance is committed as loan collateral (Section 3.4.5), excluding held funds from the withdrawable balance returned by GET /api/accounts/{id}/balance.

## Loan and Credit Management Subsystem

This subsystem owns the Loan and LoanRepayment classes. It implements loan application, automated eligibility assessment against the savings multiplier and guarantor pledges, approval workflow, disbursement, interest calculation, and repayment tracking through endpoints such as POST /api/loan-applications, POST /api/loans/{id}/approve, and POST /api/loans/{id}/disburse. It depends on the Savings and Shares subsystem to verify a member\'s savings balance and guarantor pledges before approving a loan, and on Member Management to confirm the applicant\'s standing.

## Document and Reporting Engine

This subsystem reads data from the other subsystems on demand rather than owning its own persistent business classes. Document generation produces member-facing artifacts (statements, loan agreements, receipts, share certificates) from a standardized template, triggered by a specific transaction or member request through an endpoint such as GET /api/members/{id}/statement. Reporting aggregates ledger data across members, accounts, and loans to produce financial and operational reports for management and auditors.

## Security and Audit Access Control Subsystem

This subsystem enforces the RBAC security matrix defined in Section 2.6.2 across every REST endpoint via a guard-based authorization middleware in the NestJS request pipeline, rather than operating as a separate module a user interacts with directly. It authenticates staff accounts (issuing a signed token on login), associates each account with a role, and rejects any request whose role is not authorized for the target endpoint before the request reaches the relevant subsystem\'s business logic. It also owns the audit log: every create, update, and approval action performed by another subsystem is recorded with a timestamp and the acting user\'s identity.

## Channel Integration Subsystem

This subsystem is the boundary between the Application Logic Layer and each client channel. For the Next.js Web Application, it serves the REST API directly over HTTPS with JSON payloads. For the \*812# USSD channel, it exposes a dedicated controller that accepts the USSD gateway\'s session callback (typically an HTTP POST containing the session ID, the member\'s phone number, and the current menu input), maps that input to the appropriate read-only call against Member Management or Savings and Shares, and returns a plain-text response formatted within the character and session-timeout limits described in Section 1.1.9, rather than a JSON payload intended for the dashboard.

## Double-Entry Transaction Ledger Logic

To guarantee accounting integrity, every monetary movement in the system is recorded as a balanced pair of ledger entries rather than as a single balance adjustment. A member deposit, for instance, debits a cash/teller control account and credits the member\'s savings account; a loan disbursement debits a loan-portfolio account and credits the member\'s savings account; a loan repayment reverses that pairing, splitting the payment between principal and accrued interest. Each Transaction record references two LedgerEntry rows rather than a single account, and the NestJS Application Logic Layer

enforces, before the Data Access Layer commits the record within a single atomic database transaction, that the sum of debits equals the sum of credits for every transaction.

system two properties a single-entry balance update cannot: any member\'s or the SACCO\'s overall financial position can be reconstructed and independently verified at any point in time by summing ledger entries, and any discrepancy in the books surfaces immediately as an out-of-balance transaction rejected outright, rather than as an unexplained gap discovered only at month-end reconciliation.

# Persistent Data Management and Relational Database Design

## Entity-Relationship Diagram

The diagram below (produced as a PlantUML Entity-Relationship script) models the ten core tables that persist the business classes identified in the refined class diagram (Section 2.4.1): members, accounts, loans, loan_repayments, loan_guarantors, transactions, ledger_entries, staff_accounts, roles_permissions, and audit_logs. Each entity is drawn with its primary key, its foreign keys, and its typed attributes, using crow\'s-foot cardinality notation on every connector.

Member is the root entity: a single member holds one-to-many accounts, may apply for one-to-many loans, and may separately stand as guarantor on one-to-many loan_guarantors records. Each loan carries a one-to-many relationship to loan_repayments (its amortization schedule) and to loan_guarantors (its collateral pledges), and an optional one-to-many relationship to transactions, capturing every disbursement and repayment posted against that loan (a loan may have zero transactions before disbursement, and many afterward). The updated schema also gives accounts a direct, optional one-to-many \"originates\" relationship to transactions: a non-loan savings deposit or withdrawal now links straight to the account it moves money against through transactions.account_id, rather than being reachable only indirectly through ledger_entries. Symmetrically, loan_repayments carries an optional one-to-one \"settles\" relationship to transactions through a new transaction_id foreign key, so that a scheduled installment can be traced forward to the specific posted transaction (a full or partial payment) that settled it, and a transaction can be traced backward to the installment it discharged. Every transaction, whichever of these two paths originated it, still resolves into exactly two balanced ledger_entries rows --- the debit leg and the credit leg --- which is what gives the schema its double-entry integrity property (Section 3.2.8). On the administrative side, roles_permissions grants one role to many staff_accounts, and each staff_accounts record in turn approves many loans, records many transactions, and generates many audit_logs entries; audit_logs.staff_id is optional, since background and system-automated processes (a USSD auto-session, an interest batch run) must also be able to write an audit trail entry without an attributable human staff user. This relationship structure is exactly the one enforced at the schema level in Section 3.4.4 and detailed field-by-field in the Data Dictionary in Section 3.4.3.

## Referential Integrity, Keys, and Indexing Strategy

Every foreign key relationship in the schema is enforced by PostgreSQL itself rather than left to application-level checks alone, so that a loan record can never reference a nonexistent member and a ledger entry can never post against a closed or nonexistent account. Deletion of a Member or Account record uses ON DELETE RESTRICT wherever transaction history depends on it, since SACCO records must remain retrievable for audit purposes even after an account is closed; a status flag, not a row deletion, marks an account or member as inactive. Beyond the primary and foreign keys listed above, the schema indexes fayda_national_id and member name (member lookup), posted_date on ledger_entries (statement and report generation), and status on loans (the loan officer\'s and manager\'s review queues); every one of these indexes is additionally composed with tenant_id as its leading column, since every query in a multi-tenant, Row-Level-Security-scoped table filters on tenant_id first, and a tenant-scoped index keeps that filter efficient rather than forcing a full-table scan followed by an RLS filter.

## Guarantor and Collateral Logic

Most SACCO lending policies require a loan to be secured either by the borrower\'s own savings balance under the savings-multiplier rule, or by one or more guarantors who pledge a portion of their own savings against the loan. The schema represents this with a loan_guarantors table (loan_guarantor_id PK; loan_id and guarantor_member_id and pledged_account_id as foreign keys; pledged_amount BIGINT, stored as integer minor units)). This design supports several business rules that the Loan and Credit Management subsystem enforces before a loan is approved or disbursed:

-   A guarantor\'s total pledged amount across all loans they currently guarantee, plus any new pledge, must not exceed their unencumbered (unpledged) savings balance.

-   Approving a loan places a hold on the pledged portion of each guarantor\'s savings account (accounts.held_balance), which the Savings and Shares subsystem enforces by excluding held funds from the withdrawable balance shown to the teller.

-   A loan may carry multiple guarantors, whose pledged amounts sum, together with the member\'s own savings-multiplier coverage, to at least the collateral coverage required by SACCO policy for that loan\'s principal.

-   Full repayment of the loan releases the hold on every associated guarantor pledge automatically, restoring the pledged funds to the guarantor\'s withdrawable balance.

-   Default on the loan triggers a defined recovery flow in which the pledged guarantor amount is applied against the outstanding balance before any other collections action, recorded as its own set of balanced ledger entries per Section 3.2.8.

# Network and Deployment Architecture

## Logical Deployment View

The system deploys as a single, shared multi-tenant hosting arrangement rather than one deployment per SACCO, mirroring the software architecture in Section 3.1.3. A pool of NestJS Application Logic Layer instances, run behind a load balancer as a single horizontally scalable application-server cluster, serves every tenant SACCO from the same running codebase and the same deployed version; there is no dedicated virtual machine, container, or database provisioned per tenant. A client browser, used by tellers, loan officers, managers, and auditors of any tenant SACCO, communicates over HTTPS with this shared application-server cluster, which in turn communicates with a single shared PostgreSQL Database Server hosting the schema described in Section 3.4, where every tenant\'s rows coexist in the same tables and are separated only by the tenant_id-scoped Row-Level Security policies described in Section 2.6.6, not by physical or schema-level separation. In parallel, a member of any tenant SACCO dials \*812# on a GSM handset; the mobile network operator routes this to a licensed USSD aggregator/gateway, which forwards each menu interaction as an HTTP callback to the same shared application-server cluster\'s Channel Integration subsystem, which resolves the calling member\'s tenant from the dialed number before serving the request. Onboarding a new SACCO onto the platform is a configuration and data-provisioning act rather than an infrastructure-provisioning one: the Super Admin actor (Section 2.1.1.5) creates a new tenant_id and binds its Row-Level Security policies through the Super Admin Console, after which the new tenant\'s staff can authenticate and begin operating immediately on the existing, already-running application and database instances, with no server to procure, provision, or configure for that tenant specifically.

Access from client browsers to the application-server cluster is over public HTTPS with proper certificate management, since tenant staff and members may connect from any tenant SACCO\'s office or from a mobile device rather than a single institution\'s local area network; authentication and the RBAC matrix (Section 2.6.2), not network-perimeter restriction, are the primary access control for this shared, internet-facing deployment. The USSD Gateway connection is authenticated with credentials issued by the aggregator and restricted, at the firewall, to the aggregator\'s published IP range, so that the

/api/v1/ussd/session endpoint cannot be called by an arbitrary external client. Because the application-server cluster and database are shared across every tenant, capacity planning and scaling decisions are made once for the platform as a whole rather than separately per SACCO, and a spike in one tenant\'s usage (for example, month-end reporting) is absorbed by the shared cluster\'s horizontal scaling rather than requiring that tenant\'s own dedicated hardware to be resized.

## Backup and Disaster Recovery Strategy

Because the PostgreSQL database is the SACCO\'s single source of truth for member funds, its protection against loss is a design requirement, not an operational afterthought. The system adopts a layered backup strategy suited to a small-to-medium SACCO\'s resources:

-   A full pg_dump database backup runs on an automated nightly schedule, with the resulting file transferred to storage physically or geographically separate from the primary server.

-   PostgreSQL Write-Ahead Log (WAL) archiving runs continuously between full backups, enabling point-in-time recovery to within minutes of a failure rather than losing an entire day\'s ledger transactions.

-   Backup retention follows a simple rotation (seven daily, four weekly, twelve monthly copies) that bounds storage growth while still allowing recovery from an error discovered well after it occurred.

-   Restore procedures are tested periodically on a non-production copy of the system, since an untested backup carries no real assurance of recoverability; because every tenant shares the same database, a full pg_dump restore recovers every tenant SACCO together, and a single tenant\'s data cannot be restored in isolation from a full-database backup without a separate, tenant-filtered logical export taken alongside it.

-   Where the primary server is hosted locally rather than in the cloud, it is placed behind an uninterruptible power supply (UPS) to prevent data corruption from an abrupt power loss during an atomic ledger-posting transaction.

# Chapter Four: Frontend Architecture & User Interface Design {#chapter-four-frontend-architecture-user-interface-design .unnumbered}

This chapter presents the frontend architecture and user experience design for the Enterprise SACCO Management SaaS Platform Where the preceding backend architecture chapter established the Clean/Multi-Layered Architecture layering, the NestJS REST API, and the shared-database, tenant_id-scoped multi-tenancy model secured with PostgreSQL Row-Level Security, this chapter addresses how the client experience is built to consume that API surface reliably --- across four distinct user roles, in environments ranging from head-office broadband to poorly connected rural branches, and during the sensitive one-time process of onboarding a SACCO\'s legacy records into the platform

# Tech Stack Selection

The frontend stack was selected to satisfy four simultaneous constraints that are typical of financial SaaS products: strict type safety around monetary values, fast perceived performance for teller-facingscreens, a consistent design system across four very different role-based interfaces, and long-term maintainability by a team that must extend the product without regressing existing tenants

## Next.js (App Router)

Next.js was chosen as the application framework, built on its App Router paradigm rather than thelegacy Pages Router The App Router\'s nested layouts map naturally onto the platform\'s tenancy model: a root layout resolves the active tenant and authenticated session once, while nested route groups scope Super Admin, Tenant Admin, Teller Desk, and Member Portal experiences into independent layout trees that share only what is common (authentication context, theming, and the API client) Server Components are used for read-heavy, low-interactivity screens such as report listings and audit views, reducing client-side JavaScript and improving first paint on the low-bandwidth branch connections the platform must support Client Components are reserved for interactive surfaces --- transaction entry forms, the teller desk, and the CSV mapping wizard --- where local state and optimistic updates are required

## TypeScript

TypeScript is enforced across the codebase in strict mode Domain and API contract types are generated directly from the backend\'s OpenAPI specification, so that a change to a DTO on the NestJS side (for example, a new field on a savings transaction) produces a compile-time error on the frontend rather than a runtime surprise discovered by a teller mid-transaction Monetary amounts are never

represented as native floating-point numbers in application state; they are modelled as minor-unit integers (cents) with a dedicated Money value type that mirrors the backend\'s decimal handling, and all arithmetic on amounts is routed through this type to prevent rounding defects

## Tailwind CSS and Shadcn UI

Tailwind CSS supplies the utility-first styling layer, while Shadcn UI provides accessible, unstyled-by-default component primitives (dialogs, comboboxes, data tables, toasts) that are copied into the codebase rather than imported as an opaque dependency This ownership model matters for a multi-role SACCO product: components can be themed per tenant (logo, primary colour, and typography scale drawn from tenant configuration) without forking a third-party library, and accessibility behaviour (focus trapping, keyboard navigation, ARIA roles) is inherited rather than re-implemented for every new screen A shared design-token layer (spacing, colour, radius, and elevation scales) is defined once and consumed by all four role-based interfaces, ensuring visual consistency even though each interface exposes a very different feature set

## React Query (TanStack Query)

All communication with the NestJS REST API is mediated through TanStack Query rather than ad hoc fetch calls or a global Redux store TanStack Query owns server-state concerns that are otherwise easy to get wrong in a financial product: request de-duplication, background refetching, cache invalidation keyed by tenant and entity, retry policies with exponential backoff for transient network failures, and stale-time configuration tuned per data type (member balances are treated as more volatile than, say, a chart-of-accounts listing). Mutations --- deposits, withdrawals, loan disbursement requests --- are implemented as TanStack Query mutations with explicit

\`onMutate\`/\`onError\`/\`onSettled\` lifecycles, which is the mechanism that underpins the optimistic-update behaviour described in Section 4.2

# Architecture & State Management

## Client-Side Routing and Layout Composition

Routing follows a role-first, tenant-second segmentation: the URL structure nests a resolved tenant slug beneath a role-scoped route group (for example, a teller\'s deposit screen and a member\'s statement view are physically separate route trees even though both eventually call related savings endpoints) This separation allows each interface to be code-split independently --- a Teller Desk session never downloads the JavaScript bundle for the Super Admin console, which materially reduces load time on branch hardware Route-level middleware re-validates the session\'s role claim on every navigation, so a role change on the backend (for example, a teller being demoted) takes effect on the very next client-side transition rather than only at next login

## Optimistic UI Updates for Financial Transactions

Teller-facing transactions are the platform\'s highest-frequency, highest-stakes interactions, and the interface is designed to feel instantaneous even though every transaction must ultimately be confirmed and posted by the backend\'s ledger engine When a teller submits a deposit or withdrawal, the

UI immediately reflects the expected post-transaction balance and appends a pending-state row to the member\'s transaction list, using TanStack Query\'s cache-write APIs to patch local state ahead of the server response The pending row is visually distinguished (a subdued colour and a small clock indicator) so tellers are never misled into treating an unconfirmed transaction as settled If the backend rejects the transaction --- insufficient funds, a concurrency conflict, or a validation failure surfaced by the ledger --- the optimistic patch is rolled back atomically and a specific, actionable error is shown (for example, distinguishing an overdrawn account from a network timeout), rather than a generic failure toast This pattern is deliberately not applied to loan disbursement or account-closure actions, which involve larger, less reversible sums and instead use a pessimistic pattern with an explicit loading and confirmation state

## Offline-First Capabilities for Remote Branch Tellers

Many SACCO branches operate on intermittent GSM-modem or satellite connectivity Ratherthan treating connectivity loss as an error state, the Teller Desk interface is built offline-first using IndexedDB as a local durable store, accessed through a thin repository layer that mirrors the shape of the backend\'s API responses Reference data required for teller operations --- member records, product rates, and the tenant\'s chart of accounts --- is synchronised into IndexedDB on login and refreshed opportunistically whenever connectivity is available Transactions entered while offline are written to an IndexedDB outbox queue with a locally generated idempotency key, a client-side timestamp, and a monotonic sequence number scoped to the teller\'s device A background synchronisation worker, built on the Service Worker Background Sync API where supported and a polling fallback otherwise, drains this outbox against the backend whenever a connection becomes available, submitting queued transactions in original sequence order and using the idempotency key so that a retried submission (for example, after a partial network failure) cannot be posted twice The teller-facing UI surfaces an explicit, persistent connectivity and sync-queue indicator --- showing the count of transactions awaiting synchronisation --- so that branch staff always know whether a transaction has genuinely reached the ledger or is still local to the device Conflicts that can only be resolved server-side (for instance, two offline tellers both attempting a withdrawal that would jointly overdraw an account) are rejected individually by the backend on sync and routed back to the originating device as a targeted, reviewable exception rather than a silent failure

# User Experience & Role-Based Layouts

The platform exposes four structurally distinct interfaces from a shared component and design-token library Each is scoped to the minimum functionality its role requires, both for usability and as a frontend-level reinforcement of the RBAC boundaries enforced by the backend

## Super Admin Console

The Super Admin interface operates above the tenant boundary and is used by the platform operator rather than any individual SACCO Its primary surfaces are tenant provisioning (creating a new SACCO\'s tenant_id record, initial RLS policy binding, and configuration within the shared PostgreSQL database), cross-tenant health and usage dashboards, subscription and billing state, platform-wide feature-flag management, and impersonation tooling for support purposes --- the latter gated behind step-up authentication and fully captured in the audit trail described in Section 5.2 Because this console can affect every tenant on the platform, destructive or tenant-provisioning actions require an explicit confirmation dialog that echoes back the tenant name the operator is about to modify

## Tenant / SACCO Admin Dashboard

The Tenant Admin dashboard is the operational home for a SACCO\'s management staff It surfaces configuration that is scoped entirely within the tenant\'s own database: product and interest-

rate setup, branch and staff management, approval workflows for loans above a configurable threshold, and tenant-level financial reporting (trial balance, member growth, portfolio-at-risk) Layout emphasizes dashboard-style summary cards and drill-down tables over data entry, reflecting that this role reviews and approves more often than it originates transactions

## Teller Desk

The Teller Desk is the highest-throughput interface and is deliberately minimal: a single-column, keyboard-first layout optimised for a teller processing dozens of transactions per hour, often on modest branch hardware Member lookup, deposit, withdrawal, and loan-repayment actions are reachable within one or two clicks or key presses from the desk\'s home screen, and the layout keeps the offline-sync indicator described in Section 4.2.3 permanently visible Numeric entry uses a large, high-contrast amount field with immediate on-screen recalculation of the resulting balance, reducing keying errors at the point of cash handling

## Member Self-Service Portal

The Member Portal is the public-facing, mobile-responsive surface through which individual SACCO members view balances and statements, submit loan applications, and manage their own profile and notification preferences Its design favours a simplified, card-based information hierarchy suited to members with varying levels of digital literacy, progressive disclosure of detail (a summary balance first, with statement detail one tap away), and full responsiveness down to small Android handsets, which represent the majority of the platform\'s expected member-side traffic

# Legacy Data Onboarding Module

Every newly onboarded SACCO arrives with an existing book of members, savings balances, and loan accounts, typically held in spreadsheets or an aging desktop system The onboarding module is a guided, multi-step workflow that turns this unstructured legacy data into a validated, ledger-consistent opening position scoped to the new tenant\'s tenant_id within the shared PostgreSQL database, without requiring engineering involvement for each new customer This bulk-import path is distinct from, and complementary to, the real-time Fayda National ID verification flow described in Chapters One through Three: the Legacy Member Onboarding Import DTO in Section 4.2.4 carries forward whatever national-ID value a tenant\'s prior records already contain, but does not itself call the Fayda verification service, since a historical member\'s identity was established under the SACCO\'s previous process. Once imported, that member\'s profile is flagged for verification-on-next-visit, so the first time the member (or a teller on their behalf) transacts against the migrated account, the standard Register Member /

identity-confirmation flow calls the Fayda National ID service to attach a verified national ID going forward, converging the legacy record onto the same identity-assurance standard applied to every newly registered member.

## Import and Schema Mapping

The workflow begins with a file upload step accepting CSV and Excel workbooks The platform parses the header row and presents a schema-mapping screen in which the onboarding operator aligns each source column to a target field in the platform\'s member, savings, or loan schema (for example, mapping a legacy "Acc_No" column to the platform\'s member account identifier) Previously used mappings are saved per tenant and offered as a suggested template on subsequent imports, since a SACCO is typically migrating several related files --- members, savings ledgers, and loan schedules --- that share column conventions

## Staging and Validation

Mapped rows are written to a tenant-scoped staging area rather than committed directly to live tables A validation pass then runs a configurable rule set against the staged data: required-field completeness, duplicate national-ID or account-number detection, referential checks (a loan row referencing a member ID that does not appear in the member import), and financial consistency checks (a member\'s opening savings balance reconciling against any provided transaction history)

Results are presented as a row-level report, colour-coded by severity, allowing the operator to correct source data and re-upload only the affected rows rather than restarting the entire import

## Commit and Reconciliation

Once staged data passes validation, the operator reviews a summary screen --- member count, aggregate opening savings balance, aggregate outstanding loan principal --- before triggering the commit step Committing is implemented as a single backend transaction per tenant that both creates the member, savings, and loan records and posts the corresponding opening balances through the ledger engine as day-one journal entries, so that legacy balances enter the double-entry system the same way any other transaction would, rather than as an unaudited data patch A post-commit reconciliation report is generated automatically, comparing the committed totals against the pre-commit staging summary, giving the onboarding SACCO\'s finance team a concrete artefact to sign off against their prior records

# Chapter Five: External Integrations, Security, & Delivery Schedule {#chapter-five-external-integrations-security-delivery-schedule .unnumbered}

This chapter completes the frontend-facing view of the platform by addressing the boundary between the application and the outside world: the mobile money and messaging gateways members and tellers interact with, the frontend-side security controls that protect the NestJS REST API\'s authentication and authorisation model in the browser, and the delivery schedule that brings frontend, integration, and backend work streams to a coordinated release

# Integration Contracts & Ecosystem

## Mobile Money Integration --- C2B and B2C

The platform integrates with mobile money and banking gateways (M-Pesa, Telebirr, and partner bank channels) across two distinct transaction directions, each with its own frontend workflow and state machine

-   Customer-to-Business (C2B): A member initiates a savings deposit or loan repayment from their mobile money wallet The frontend generates a payment request --- typically a reference code or STK/USSD push, depending on the gateway --- and then transitions the initiating screen (Teller Desk or Member Portal) into a pending state, polling a lightweight status endpoint until the corresponding webhook has been received and processed by the backend, or until a configurable timeout is reached, at which point the member is guided to retry or contact support

-   Business-to-Customer (B2C): The platform disburses funds outward, most commonly for loan disbursement or savings withdrawal to mobile wallet Because B2C moves funds out of the SACCO, the Tenant Admin interface requires an explicit approval step before a disbursement request is sent to the gateway, and the resulting status (accepted, queued, failed) is surfaced back into the same approval screen rather than a separate notifications page, keeping the audit trail of who approved what visually contiguous with the outcome

Both flows are modelled on the frontend as explicit state machines (initiated → pending → confirmed / failed / reversed) rather than a single boolean success flag, since mobile money gateways in the target markets can report intermediate and delayed states that a simpler model would misrepresent to the teller or member

## Event-Driven Webhooks and Domain Event Mapping

Inbound payment-gateway webhooks are received and verified by the backend, but the frontend\'s integration layer defines the contract each webhook must ultimately produce for the client to consume:

a typed domain event (for example, a SavingsDepositedEvent or LoanDisbursedEvent) with a stable shape, regardless of which underlying gateway triggered it This normalisation matters because the platform supports multiple mobile money providers per tenant, each with a different raw webhook payload; by mapping every provider\'s callback to the same small set of domain events, the Teller Desk, Member Portal, and notification pipeline only ever need to understand the normalised event, not each gateway\'s idiosyncratic format On the client, these events arrive over a lightweight real-time channel (WebSocket or Server-Sent Events) scoped to the relevant tenant and member, which is what allows the C2B polling described above to resolve immediately when the corresponding event arrives, rather than waiting for the next poll interval

Current-phase implementation note: the SMS/WhatsApp gateway architecture below is the target production design. In the current internship phase, transactional notifications are implemented by email only, via Nodemailer over SMTP (Gmail SMTP for pilot/demo volumes), because SMS/WhatsApp aggregator access was not pursued for this phase. The wrapper-service pattern described below (a single internal send interface, decoupled from the underlying provider) is the same pattern used for the email implementation, so migrating to SMS/WhatsApp later is a provider swap behind the existing interface rather than a redesign.

## SMS & WhatsApp Notification Gateways

Transactional notifications --- deposit and withdrawal receipts, loan approval and disbursement confirmations, and one-time passwords for step-up authentication --- are sent through thin, provider-agnostic gateway wrapper services Each wrapper exposes a single internal interface (send transactional message, send OTP) and maps it to the specific request format of the underlying SMS aggregator or WhatsApp Business API provider, so that a tenant can be switched to an alternative provider, or a provider outage can trigger an automatic fallback from WhatsApp to SMS, without any change to the calling code in the Teller Desk or Member Portal Message templates are stored per tenant and per notification type, allowing a SACCO to localise wording (including language) without a code change or redeployment

# End-to-End Security & Compliance

## Frontend JWT Handling and Token Storage

The platform authenticates against the NestJS backend\'s identity endpoints and receives a short-lived JWT access token together with a longer-lived refresh token Access tokens are held only in memory (an in-page store, not persisted storage), and are attached to API requests via an Authorization header injected by the TanStack Query/API-client layer The refresh token is stored in an HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict cookie set by the backend, which means it is never directly readable or writable by client-side JavaScript and is therefore not a viable target for a cross-site-scripting exfiltration attempt Deliberately, no token of any kind is written to localStorage or sessionStorage: those APIs are readable by any script running on the page, and a financial application handling member funds treats that as an unacceptable exposure surface Silent token refresh is performed via a background call that the browser attaches the HttpOnly cookie to automatically, refreshing the in-memory access token shortly before expiry so that an active teller session is never interrupted mid-transaction

## Dynamic RBAC Route Guards

Role and permission claims are decoded from the access token (and revalidated against the backend on each navigation, as noted in Section 4.2.1) and drive a client-side route-guard layer that sits in front of every role-scoped route group A user without the required claim for a route is redirected before any route-specific component or data-fetching logic executes, rather than being shown a partially rendered screen and then an error Because the guard configuration is data-driven --- a declarative map of route pattern to required permission --- new roles or permission granularity introduced on the backend can be reflected on the frontend by updating configuration rather than rewriting guard logic This client-side guard is treated strictly as a user-experience convenience; every guarded action is independently re-authorised by the backend\'s own RBAC enforcement, so a bypassed or misconfigured client guard can degrade the experience but cannot by itself grant unauthorised access to data or transactions
